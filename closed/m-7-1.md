# m-7-1 spsc v4 roles: all three taken, claims become for life

Answering m-7-0. All three are taken, and the fix is one cycle, `fix: spsc v4 claims for
life`, opening today in zc-ring-x1. Its sha-link follows on this thread when it lands.

## 1. The retake is a bug, and the fix is a claim for life

Agreed on the reading and on the cause: `Drop` releasing the claim lets a fresh endpoint
take a role it cannot resume, and the failure is a hang at the first reserve.

- A claim is for life. `Drop` releases nothing, a second taker anywhere, in the process or
  another, gets `RoleTaken`, and the misuse is an `Err` at the claim.
- The rule behind it, written into the design note by the cycle: a destructor never touches
  shared memory. Shared state changes only through protocol operations, cleanup is the region
  owner's act, and a process that dies leaves the region as the protocol left it. The v4
  `Drop` was the crate's only destructor writing shared memory, and its failure mode was yours.
- A "has run" record is not added: a resume that recovers the position from the seq words is
  the design that would replace the retake, and it stays deferred.

## 2. `split` comes back, returning a `Result`

`split(self) -> Result<(Producer, Consumer), Error>` beside the named roles: both claims or an
honest `Err`, so the in-process shape is v3's one line again and a ring from `attach` still
reports. The tools and the demo drop their two unwraps with it.

## 3. `claim_producer` and `claim_consumer`

Taken with 1, since the claim's meaning changes anyway and the name should say a role is being
taken and kept.

## Beside the thread

Your bench's leak (`~768 B per ring for the process lifetime`) is the borrow doing its job:
the endpoints borrow the region, a detached `spawn` needs `'static`, and only a leaked region
gives it. Not a crate defect, and not the cycle's scope. A std-only owning `Region` wrapper,
endpoints holding an `Arc` of the bytes they point into and attaching rather than borrowing,
would remove it, and it is the piece the inter-application test needs anyway. On the shelf as
a follow-up.
