# m-7-2 spsc v4 roles: correcting m-7-1, no split, and roles survive their holders

Correcting m-7-1, whose plan changed before any code did. Two of its answers are withdrawn, and
the fix is now the cycle `fix: spsc v4 roles survive their holders`, open in zc-ring-x1, its
sha-link to follow on this thread when it lands.

## 1. Not a claim for life, a named and checkpointed claim

The retake is still a bug and the cause is still `Drop`. What changed is the requirement: a
process that holds a role and crashes is replaced, and its replacement continues the ring. A
claim for life answers the retake and fails that, since a holder that never releases strands its
role for good.

- A claim names its holder: `claim_producer(id)` and `claim_consumer(id)`, the id the app's,
  never interpreted by the crate. A held role is `Err(RoleTaken)`.
- `Drop` writes nothing, as m-7-1 said, and the rule stands: a destructor never touches shared
  memory.
- `release(self)` on either endpoint writes an exact checkpoint into the region and parks the
  role, and a later claim, in this process or another, resumes from it.
- Endpoints also checkpoint at every segment switch, one or two stores on a path that already
  writes shared memory, and nothing on the message path.
- `take_over_producer(id)` and `take_over_consumer(id)` replace a held claim, the caller vouching
  the holder is gone: they load the switch-time checkpoint and scan one segment's seq words for
  the position. A replacement consumer loses nothing, and a replacement producer at most the one
  slot the dead one had reserved and not committed.
- Judging that a holder is dead stays out of the crate: the id and the takeover are the app's,
  a supervisor's call.

## 2. `split` is refused

Withdrawn. The shape the crate serves is multi-process: each app creates the ring it reads and
joins the other's as producer, so no process holds both roles of one ring, and a call claiming
both serves a case that shape never has. The two claims are the API, in-process and across
processes, and in-process callers keep their two `unwrap`s, each with its `// OK` comment.

## 3. The names

Unchanged from m-7-1: `claim_producer` and `claim_consumer`, with `release` and `take_over_*`
beside them.
